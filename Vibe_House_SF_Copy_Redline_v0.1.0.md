# Vibe House SF — Proposed Website Copy

**Status:** Editorial draft for review. Nothing in this document has been applied to the website.

This document replaces abstract recommendations with ready-to-paste copy. The aim is to make Vibe House feel more specific, more human, and more useful to the people it is for: **exited founders who want to stay close to the work, the ideas, and one another.**

## Editorial Direction

Vibe House should feel like a real house with a real weekly rhythm, not a generic founder community or wellness brand. The copy should make three things clear quickly: the room is for people who have been through an exit; the work inside is practical and current, especially around AI; and the house has been intentionally shaped for conversation, attention, and long creative sessions.

The proposed language avoids broad promises about health, performance, or AI. It keeps the house’s unusual character while sounding less like a manifesto and more like a place a discerning founder would want to visit.

## Hero

### Definition

**Current**

> A new type of hacker house for exited founders to vibe code together.

**Proposed**

> San Francisco’s clubhouse for exited founders who still want to make things—together.

**Why this is stronger**

It establishes the audience, the location, and the post-exit impulse without relying on generic “new type of” language or making “vibe coding” the entire proposition.

### Primary CTA

**Current**

> Join our next event

**Proposed**

> See what’s happening at the house

**Secondary CTA**

> Learn how the house works

**Why this is stronger**

The primary action feels less like a generic signup and more like an invitation to explore a real program. The final form CTA can remain **Join our next event**.

## The Concept

**Current**

> Show up any day we’re open. Vibe code on your own project. Share what’s working — the agents, the prompts, the tricks. We also have an ongoing group project that everyone prompts together.

**Proposed**

> Bring the project that has been sitting in the back of your mind. Bring the workflow that is almost working. Bring the question you cannot answer from another feed.
>
> At Vibe House, people work alongside one another, compare the tools and methods they are actually using, and make time for the conversations that usually happen after the meeting ends. Some days are quiet. Some become impromptu demos. Most end with someone showing the room what changed.

**Why this is stronger**

It describes the lived experience of the house rather than listing AI vocabulary. It also makes room for solo work, shared practice, demos, and dinner without overexplaining any one format.

## Why Now

**Current**

> The tools are evolving daily. The founders who master them first will define what comes next.

**Proposed**

> AI is changing the craft faster than any one person can track. The advantage is not simply finding the newest tool. It is seeing how capable people use it, where it breaks, and what becomes possible when the right people compare notes in the same room.

**Why this is stronger**

It explains why Vibe House exists instead of repeating a broad claim about fast-moving tools.

## The Vibe

### Section introduction

**Current**

> Every detail engineered for deep focus and peak performance.

**Proposed**

> The house is designed for long, clear-headed days: natural light, places to sit or stand, thoughtful food and tea, good sound, and enough calm to stay with a difficult problem. Nothing here is meant to perform wellness. It is meant to make the room easier to inhabit.

**Why this is stronger**

It keeps the health-conscious positioning while removing unsupported performance promises.

# `/why` — Why Now

## Hero introduction

**Current**

> The story of why we built Vibe House — and why this moment in time is unlike anything we’ve seen before.

**Proposed**

> The exit gives you something rare: room to choose what matters without a board meeting, a runway, or the next round dictating the answer. AI has made that freedom more immediate. A half-formed idea can become something you can touch before the week is over. The question is no longer whether there is enough to explore. It is how to stay close to the people who make exploration worthwhile.

## 01 — We Remember the Room

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> Before the company became a company, there was usually a room: a kitchen table, a living room, a borrowed office, a place where someone could turn around and ask, “Can you look at this?”
>
> That proximity mattered. It made ideas less precious. It made feedback faster. It made the work feel shared even when the responsibility was not. Vibe House begins with a belief that those rooms still matter.

## 02 — Success Changes the Work

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> An exit changes the texture of a founder’s life. The calendar opens. The urgent problems are no longer yours to solve. People assume that freedom should feel like an ending.
>
> For many founders, it creates a different kind of restlessness. Not the pressure to prove something, but the desire to be close to ideas again—to make something small, strange, useful, or unfinished simply because it is interesting.

## 03 — The Tools Changed the Relationship to Making

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> AI has shortened the distance between an idea and a first attempt. A founder can sketch a product, test a workflow, or interrogate an unfamiliar technical problem without assembling the team that used to stand between curiosity and action.
>
> That does not make the work easy. It makes judgment more important. Taste, context, and the ability to recognize what is worth pursuing become the scarce parts.

## 04 — Freedom Is Better With Friction

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> Post-exit freedom can be unexpectedly solitary. You can explore almost anything, which makes it easier to disappear into your own loop.
>
> The right room provides useful friction: someone asking the hard question, someone showing a better workflow, someone reminding you that an unfinished idea is allowed to stay unfinished until it becomes clear.

## 05 — You Cannot Learn This From a Feed

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> New models, products, and techniques arrive constantly. But the most useful knowledge is rarely the announcement. It is the messy part that follows: the workaround, the failure mode, the prompt that looked promising but did not survive real use.
>
> That is what people share when they are working beside one another. Not theory. Practice.

## 06 — A Different Kind of Peer Group

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> Vibe House is not a room where everyone is trying to sell the same story. It is a room where people can be candid because they have already carried some version of the weight.
>
> That shared history changes the quality of the conversation. The goal is not to impress the room. The goal is to leave with a sharper view of the thing you are trying to understand.

## 07 — The Week Needs Different Rooms

**Proposed replacement chapter**

> Some questions are technical. Some are about capital. Some are about meaning, health, identity, or what a life after the company is meant to contain.
>
> The house makes room for all of them. That is why the week has a rhythm: deeper conversations, practical working sessions, live demos, and slower mornings where no one has to perform certainty.

## 08 — Why Vibe House

**Current closing**

> So we built a house. Not for pitching. Not for networking. For building. For that feeling.

**Proposed replacement chapter and closing**

> We made a house for people who have already done the thing others are trying to do—and who still want to stay open to what comes next.
>
> Come with a project. Come with a difficult question. Come with nothing but the urge to be around people who understand both the freedom and the uncertainty of this chapter.
>
> There is no pitch to perfect. Just a room worth returning to.

**CTA**

> Visit the house

# `/events-series` — Events Series

## Page introduction

**Current**

> Every week has a rhythm. Each day carries a different energy—from philosophical depth to technical intensity to raw creative output. These are recurring gatherings for exited founders, designed around the natural cadence of a builder’s week.

**Proposed**

> The week at Vibe House is not one event repeated five times. Each gathering creates a different kind of room: one for meaning, one for capital, one for technical depth, one for unfinished work shown live, and one for the quieter questions that surface after an exit.
>
> Come for the format that meets you where you are. Stay long enough to discover the others.

## Truth Tuesdays

**Proposed theme**

> What does it mean to be human when machines can think, create, and reason? Truth Tuesdays bring exited founders, AI researchers, and thoughtful people into conversations that success does not settle: practical spirituality, multi-faith dialogue, consciousness, intimacy, and the changing relationship between people and AI.
>
> This is not a room for easy conclusions. It is a place to stay curious about how to remain grounded, humane, and connected while the tools around us become more capable.

### Vibe House Talks

**Proposed event description**

> One guest. One host. Enough time for the real conversation to appear.
>
> Vibe House Talks are long-form evenings with builders, investors, researchers, and thinkers who can speak from lived experience. There is no panel format and no rush toward a takeaway. The conversation can move from ambition and identity to technology, relationships, philosophy, and what it costs to keep choosing the work.

## Wealth Wednesdays

**Proposed theme**

> Money feels different after an exit. Wealth Wednesdays are candid conversations about raising capital, allocating it, investing with judgment, and deciding what financial freedom is actually for.
>
> The point is not to collect tactics in public. It is to compare experience honestly: what worked, what did not, and what changes when AI reshapes both the companies being funded and the people making the decisions.

### Fundraising AMA

**Proposed event description**

> A direct, practical conversation about fundraising when the obvious path is not available.
>
> Sahin Boydas shares the lessons behind raising more than $15 million across six startups from over 140 investors, including companies outside the categories investors were chasing at the time. Bring questions about timing, outreach, investor psychology, difficult categories, and the kind of conviction that survives a long process.

## Technical Thursdays

**Proposed theme**

> Technical Thursdays are for people who want to get beneath the demo. The subjects are the systems shaping the next software stack: software factories, agent orchestration, legal AI, robotics, energy, deep tech, and the architecture required to make ambitious work reliable.
>
> Expect people to bring specifics. What failed. What scaled. What was more expensive than expected. What they would do differently next time.

### Software Factory

**Proposed event description**

> Software Factory is a working series about operating AI agents in the real world. The conversation is about orchestration patterns, handoffs, supervision, reliability, cost, and the unglamorous decisions behind systems that have to keep running.
>
> The Multi-Agent Orchestration Round Table brings together people coordinating agents to produce real software—not to admire the possibility, but to compare the machinery.

### Legal AI Vibe Lab

**Proposed event description**

> Legal AI Vibe Lab is a curated working session for lawyers, legal operators, legaltech founders, investors, and AI builders who care about how agents behave in real legal workflows.
>
> Bring one workflow, one tool, or one hard question. Together, the room examines what is actually useful in contract work, compliance, discovery, legal research, invoice review, and legal operations. No vendor theater. No vague futurism. Hosted by Daniel Aydin.

## Future Fridays (Vibe Demo Nights)

**Proposed theme**

> Future Fridays are live demo nights for work that is still becoming. Exited founders bring prototypes, product pivots, agent experiments, strange side projects, and tools that may have started as a question earlier in the week.
>
> The feedback is direct because the audience understands the work. The best part is often what happens after the demo, when someone opens a laptop, offers a sharper angle, or starts working beside you.

### Vibe Night Demos

**Proposed event description**

> Vibe Night Demos is the house’s weekly rhythm: arrive, meet people, work, show what changed, and stay for dinner.
>
> The format is intentionally loose. Bring a weekend prototype, a product pivot, an AI workflow, or a problem you want the room to see. The work does not need to be polished. It needs to be real.

## Founder Friday (Breakfasts)

**Proposed theme**

> Founder Friday Breakfasts are smaller by design. Over coffee, founders who have been through an exit sit with the questions that become louder once the operating tempo changes: identity, relationships, capital, ambition, health, and the shape of the next chapter.
>
> There is no stage and no need to arrive with a conclusion. The value is in being with people who understand the terrain.

### Post-Exit Founder Breakfast

**Proposed event description**

> An intimate morning gathering for founders who have been through an acquisition, IPO, or wind-down.
>
> No pitching. No networking theater. No one working the room. Just honest conversation over coffee about what comes next: the identity shift when the calendar empties, the restlessness that arrives a few months later, the freedom to choose without pressure, and the quiet question of what to do with capital once you finally have it.

## Events CTA

**Current**

> Want to attend or host an event at Vibe House?

**Proposed**

> Interested in joining a gathering—or bringing the right conversation to the house?

**CTA**

> Tell us what you are working on

# `/biological-founder` — The Biological Founder

This page is one of the site’s most distinctive assets. It should remain personal, curious, and unconventional. The edit is not about making it clinical or bland. It is about replacing universal health claims with language that is grounded in **the house’s preferences, design choices, and lived experience**.

## Hero label

**Current**

> A Series on Health, Environment & Building

**Proposed**

> Essays on attention, environment, and the conditions around meaningful work

## Hero introduction

**Current**

> Silicon Valley knows how to optimize machines. We optimize inference speed, model performance, capital efficiency. But we rarely optimize the founder. This series explores what happens when you treat the founder’s nervous system as infrastructure.

**Proposed**

> Silicon Valley is excellent at tuning machines, metrics, and markets. It is less consistent about the conditions in which people are expected to do their clearest thinking.
>
> The Biological Founder is a series about those conditions: light, food, rest, materials, rhythm, attention, and the small choices that make a house feel more inhabitable. It is not a protocol. It is an exploration of what helps people stay present to difficult work.

## Editorial note to place below the introduction

**Proposed**

> These essays describe the practices and preferences of Vibe House. They are not medical advice, treatment guidance, or a substitute for professional care. People have different bodies, needs, beliefs, and thresholds; the house is designed to make room for that difference.

## Article I — opening replacement

**Current pattern**

> If we are building artificial intelligence to advance humanity, shouldn’t we begin by advancing ourselves?

**Proposed**

> Vibe House began with a simpler question: if people are going to spend long days thinking about the future, what kind of room makes that time feel better spent?
>
> The answer is not one thing. It is light that does not feel punishing late in the day, plants and windows that make the house feel alive, chairs and floor cushions that let people change position, meals that encourage people to stay, and enough quiet for attention to return.

## Article II — title and opening replacement

**Current title**

> Tea, Salt, and the Founder’s Immune System

**Proposed title**

> Tea, Salt, and the House Kitchen

**Proposed opening**

> In many startup houses, the kitchen is an afterthought: caffeine, packaged snacks, and whatever arrived with the last grocery order.
>
> At Vibe House, the kitchen is part of the atmosphere. There is tea worth making slowly, ingredients people recognize, and food that makes it easier to stay through the evening. The point is not to turn every meal into a theory. It is to make care visible in the ordinary parts of the day.

## Article III — opening replacement

**Current pattern**

> You can call it spiritual. You can call it atmospheric engineering.

**Proposed**

> Some people come to the house for the agents. Others notice the scent, the music, the plants, or the fact that the lights soften as the evening goes on.
>
> Vibe House does not ask everyone to share one worldview. It does make room for ritual, skepticism, humor, breath, conversation, and the small resets that help a group return to itself.

## Global line replacements for the full essay series

| Replace this pattern | With this language |
|---|---|
| “reduces airborne toxins,” “lowers cortisol,” “restores cognitive bandwidth” | “helps the house feel softer, calmer, and more alive.” |
| “protects circadian rhythm,” “regulates hormones,” “fragments thinking” | “fits the house’s preference for natural light by day and warmer light in the evening.” |
| “The kitchen is medicine” | “The kitchen is part of the house’s daily care.” |
| “The founder immune system is an asset class” | “Long projects ask a lot of a person; the house tries not to add unnecessary friction.” |
| “regulated nervous systems build better companies” | “The quality of a room can change the quality of a conversation.” |
| “performance architecture” | “an environment designed for attention, comfort, and return visits.” |
| “No processed foods / no refined sugars” repeated as a manifesto | “We try to keep the kitchen simple, ingredient-conscious, and generous.” |

# `/the-founders-pharmacy` — The Founder’s Pharmacy

## Page introduction

**Current framing**

> Every product in the house was chosen for a reason. This page tells the story of why.

**Proposed**

> The Founder’s Pharmacy is a living record of the things that make Vibe House feel like Vibe House: tea, kitchen tools, books, soap, light, scents, bedding, snacks, and the small household objects people keep asking about.
>
> This is not a prescription cabinet. It is a house list. Some items are practical, some are sensory, some are simply things we enjoy having around. We update it as the house changes.

## Editorial note to place under the introduction

**Proposed**

> Product notes describe ingredients, materials, and why an item is used at the house. They are not medical advice or claims about diagnosis, treatment, prevention, or individual outcomes.

## Category introductions

### Essential Oils & Aromatherapy

**Current**

> Scent is the most primal sense—it bypasses cognition and rewires the nervous system directly. We stock therapeutic-grade essential oils not as luxury, but as infrastructure for focus, calm, and creative flow.

**Proposed**

> Scent changes the feel of a room quickly. We keep a small selection of essential oils because people enjoy the rituals around them: a quieter start to the day, a reset between conversations, or a scent that makes the house feel familiar when you walk in.

### Teas, Coffee & Herbal Infusions

**Current**

> Tea is not a beverage—it is a ritual of presence. Every cup is a micro-meditation. The compounds in these teas—L-theanine, polyphenols, adaptogens—sharpen the mind while protecting the body.

**Proposed**

> The tea shelf is one of the social centers of the house. Some people want black tea, some want herbal tea, some want coffee, and some just need a reason to step away from the screen for five minutes. The selection is about flavor, ritual, and having something worth offering another person.

### Supplements & Superfoods

**Current**

> We do not do pills—we do whole-food nutrition in concentrated form. Every supplement in the house is chosen for bioavailability, clean ingredients, and evidence-based formulation.

**Proposed**

> This shelf contains products guests have asked about and items the house keeps on hand. We favor clear labels, familiar ingredients, and brands people can evaluate for themselves. Individual needs vary, so the list is a starting point for personal research—not a recommendation for everyone.

### Functional Beverages

**Current**

> Every drink in the house earns its place. Prebiotic sodas, adaptogen seltzers, real-fruit sparkling water—beverages that nourish the gut, sharpen the mind, or calm the nervous system.

**Proposed**

> The house has more options than coffee and canned energy drinks. These are drinks people reach for when they want something lighter, less sweet, or simply different during a long afternoon.

## Product-description standard

Replace broad health claims in individual product cards with observable, product-level descriptions.

| Current style | Proposed style |
|---|---|
| “Clinically shown to improve memory and cognitive performance.” | “A bright, herbal rosemary oil used in diffusers and room rituals.” |
| “Powerful liver detoxifier.” | “A roasted herbal tea with an earthy, bitter profile.” |
| “Reduces anxiety and normalizes stress hormone levels.” | “A fresh citrus scent selected for its clean, familiar character.” |
| “Improves insulin sensitivity and blood sugar regulation.” | “A fragrant Ceylon cinnamon used in tea, coffee, and kitchen experiments.” |
| “Promotes deep sleep without grogginess.” | “A nighttime product included for guests who prefer non-caffeinated evening routines.” |

## Closing note

**Current**

> This catalog is a living document. As we discover better products, we update the house—and this page. The biological founder never stops optimizing the environment.

**Proposed**

> This list changes as the house changes. If something becomes a favorite, we add it. If something no longer earns its place, we take it out. The best parts of a house are rarely fixed.

# `/brand` — Brand Guidelines

## Brand narrative

**Proposed addition**

> **Vibe House SF is a clubhouse for exited founders who still want to stay close to the work.**
>
> The brand should feel warm, intelligent, a little unconventional, and fully grounded. It is never corporate, never aspirational for its own sake, and never interested in networking theater. We write as people who value craft, hospitality, clear thinking, and the freedom to explore without performing certainty.

## Voice principles

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Concrete details from the house: demos, dinner, tea, laptops, questions, a room full of peers. | Generic startup language: disrupt, 10x, game-changing, the future is now. |
| “Exited founders” when describing the primary community. | “Elite,” “the best,” “the deepest,” or status language that needs to announce itself. |
| Warm, direct sentences with a point of view. | Grand claims about changing humanity, fixing wellness, or defining the future. |
| Invitations that explain what happens in the room. | Empty commands such as “Join the movement.” |

## One-sentence brand description

**Proposed**

> A San Francisco house for exited founders to work, think, demo, and stay curious together.
